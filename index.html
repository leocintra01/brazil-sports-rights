Brisanet — Restating coverage with Market Perform: balanced setup, cautious near-term view

Brisanet (BRST3) — Market Perform, TP BRL [XX] (YE27). Prices/estimates as of [XX-Aug-2026]; BRL unless noted. Draft for internal review.

Few stories in Brazilian telecom match Brisanet's: a company born in the interior of the Northeast that built, entirely organically, the region's largest fiber operation, and is now scaling a mobile business on top of it. In this context, we see a more balanced risk-reward at current levels, reflecting: i) exposure to a region with structurally challenging economics, such as relatively lower ARPU, higher churn levels, and a competitive broadband market with a high number of aggressive smaller broadband competitors; ii) a higher leverage position (~2.2x ND/EBITDA) that limits inorganic consolidation optionality and keeps interest expenses elevated amid the ongoing 5G rollout in the coming years; and iii) fewer near-term catalysts for a re-rating, considering that the Midwest expansion adds execution risk to the thesis, while the modest dividend yield (~[X]%) offers limited downside protection in a challenging macro scenario, with elevated interest rates that end up weighing on names with lower liquidity. Against this backdrop, we are restating our coverage of Brisanet with a Market Perform rating and a target price of BRL [XX]/share for 2027 (upside of [XX]%), based on a multiple of 3.5x EV/EBITDA for 2026E and [X.X]x for 2027.

A market leader built over more than two decades of regional execution. Founded more than 30 years ago to serve the interior of the Northeast, Brisanet scaled organically to become the largest ISP in the region, with over 7.2 million homes passed, ~1.6 million homes connected, and presence across every state in the region. Since then, the 2021 IPO funded both the expansion, currently with an 18.8% FTTH market share in the Northeast, and the entry into the mobile segment, following the win of 5G spectrum for the Northeast/Midwest that same year. The company has been diversifying its portfolio across broadband, mobile, TV and B2B corporate services (via the Agility franchise arm), focusing on increasing its take-up rate and bundled offerings in the coming years.

In broadband, leadership is consolidated, and the focus is shifting from footprint expansion to monetizing the existing infrastructure. Brisanet has established itself as the FTTH leader in seven of the nine Northeast states, ending 2Q26 with ~1.54 million FTTH HCs and ~7.2 million HPs, with a take-up rate of ~22% that the company now prioritizes expanding over new network build-out. Naturally, broadband is already a mature business (with higher margins than the consolidated level) with more selective marginal growth, and Brisanet has been favoring healthier ARPU levels (B2C ARPU +6.6% YoY) over faster subscriber growth, translating into softer net additions in recent months (~26k in 1H26, compared with ~67k in 1H25). Broadband churn has held broadly stable at ~2.1%, reflecting the price-sensitive consumer base and competitive dynamics of the region, while broadband revenue continues to grow at double digits despite the softer volumes, supported by the ARPU strategy and complemented by the ramp-up of the B2B corporate segment.

Mobile ramp-up is the main growth avenue, with 5G rollout obligations extending the investment cycle. Since starting mobile operations in late 2023, Brisanet has been leveraging its own 5G spectrum for the Northeast to build a convergence proposition anchored on mobile plus fiber bundles. The mobile base surpassed one million accesses by mid-2026 (1.07 million in June), and mobile gross revenue is approaching ~11% of consolidated gross revenue. However, the 5G spectrum obligations (running through 2029) require substantial capex deployment in the coming years, a trade-off that pressures free cash flow in the short term but should support operational leverage in the mid to long term.

Risks to monitor. In our view, four risks deserve closer attention: i) a competitive Northeast broadband market, still marked by relevant informal and smaller competitors, which limits pricing power; ii) the ~2.2x ND/EBITDA leverage position, which limits inorganic consolidation and keeps interest expenses elevated under a high-interest-rate macroeconomic environment; iii) capex intensity remaining high to meet the 5G rollout obligations through 2029, keeping FCF under pressure; and iv) execution risk in the mobile ramp-up outside the core Northeast footprint, particularly in the Midwest, and considering recent signs of a more promotional competitive environment in postpaid.

Brisanet offers a balanced setup, with limited near-term catalysts for a re-rating; Unifique remains as our preferred choice among ISPs. We recognize Brisanet's successful track record in the Northeast and the value of the asset built over the years. However, taking into account the execution risks associated with the Midwest expansion, a challenging macro environment, the uncertainties of the region and the company's leveraged balance sheet, we prefer to wait for a moment of greater visibility, supporting our Market Perform stance on BRST. Against this backdrop, Unifique remains our preferred choice among ISPs, considering its healthy operating metrics (churn, ARPU) in a premium region, its positioning as a potential consolidator in the South of the country, a favorable carry ([XX]% dividend yield for 2026), low leverage ([X.X]x ND/EBITDA) and the optionality of being a potential target in a scenario of consolidation in the Brazilian broadband sector.

Section 01 — Brisanet: restating coverage with Market Perform

In this section, we detail the building blocks behind our balanced stance. On the positive side, we highlight Brisanet's organic growth track record, a committed controlling family with a long-term horizon, and a mobile ramp-up that is gaining traction. On the cautious side, the structurally tougher economics of the Northeast and a maturing broadband market. Finally, we discuss the open questions that should shape the equity story ahead, from recurring capex levels to the potential of convergence.

What we like on the thesis

A rare track record of purely organic execution. Brisanet has grown entirely organically to date, becoming the largest ISP in the Northeast and expanding from 173k subscribers in small and mid-sized cities in 2018 to ~1.6 million homes connected today, with a vertically integrated model (own network, own towers, ~117k km of fiber and ~300 data centers) that supports both service quality and cost discipline. In the areas where it operates, network quality is a genuine competitive advantage, and the large incumbents have concentrated their fiber investments in denser markets, leaving the interior comparatively less contested.

What concerns us

The region's economics remain structurally more challenging. The Northeast combines a lower average ticket, a more price-sensitive consumer and monthly broadband churn of around 2.1% (compared to Unifique's 1.5%), a combination that naturally limits pricing power and makes price adjustments more gradual than in other regions. The competitive landscape adds complexity: the market remains fragmented, with a long tail of smaller ISPs competing mainly on price, a still-relevant informal segment, and regulatory enforcement that continues to evolve, particularly on pole rentals. In addition, larger players occasionally run more promotional offers for limited periods, which adds some volatility to front-book dynamics.

Broadband, the cash engine, is naturally decelerating. The broadband base is now growing ~4% YoY, down from double digits a year ago, reflecting the prioritization of healthier ARPU levels over faster subscriber growth, with marginal growth increasingly coming from take-up gains within the existing footprint. In the meantime, broadband remains the segment that funds the investment cycle: while the mobile build-out advances, capex should stay elevated (~BRL 700 million per year in 2026-27), keeping free cash flow constrained in the near term, with a gradual improvement expected as the 5G rollout matures. Beyond the required capex, there is also uncertainty around the structural margin of the business, particularly given the still-limited visibility on the mobile segment's profitability at scale.

What we do not know

  • Mobile market share in the Northeast and Midwest is the key driver of returns. The 5G investment cycle was built on the promise of convergence, and the share Brisanet ultimately reaches in both regions will define the size of that opportunity and the returns on the capital deployed; at this stage, visibility on how much the company can actually capture remains limited. The operation has already reached ~5% of the Northeast postpaid base less than three years after launch, but the pace at which convergence converts the covered population will determine where penetration ultimately settles.
  • The top-down scenario weighs more given the higher leverage. With leverage above historical levels, the thesis becomes more sensitive to the macro trajectory of the region and to the evolution of competition, variables that sit largely outside the company's control. In a scenario of interest rates staying higher for longer, financial expenses consume a larger share of cash generation and reduce the room to absorb surprises, while a faster deleveraging would restore flexibility for the company.
  • The success of the Midwest expansion remains to be demonstrated. The move will test whether the formula that worked in the Northeast can be replicated in a region where Brisanet does not yet operate and is still building local knowledge, which naturally adds uncertainty to this leg of the story.

Section 02 — Unifique: Updating estimates

Unifique continues to execute a growth agenda that keeps broadening, from consolidation to mobile, while sustaining healthy operating metrics in its core market. In this context, we are taking the opportunity to update our estimates, with a new target price of BRL [XX]/share for 2027 (upside of [XX]%), maintaining our Outperform rating. Our preference for the name among ISPs is supported by: i) a high-value proposition in a premium market, featuring a top-tier network and the lowest churn rate within our coverage; ii) a well-positioned consolidation agenda, backed by a healthy capital structure; and iii) a solid marginal growth narrative anchored on mobile plus fiber convergence, complemented by a favorable carry. On the cautious side, we monitor the ongoing investment cycle, with 5G obligations and the expansion into new regions adding execution risk to the thesis, as we detail in the blocks below.

What we like on the thesis

A differentiated value proposition in an attractive region. Unifique is the largest ISP in the South of Brazil, with a leading position in Santa Catarina (~20% broadband market share) and presence in Rio Grande do Sul and Paraná (see the table below). The company combines a top-tier network with a high-quality customer base (average ticket above local peers), which translates into the lowest FTTH churn among listed peers (~1.5% per month, versus ~2.1% for Brisanet) and strong brand recognition in its core market. In our view, this quality gap sustains pricing power and supports continued market share gains in the region.

A well-positioned potential consolidator in a fragmented market. The Brazilian ISP industry continues to move toward consolidation, and Unifique has the balance sheet to participate actively in this process (0.6x ND/EBITDA in 2Q26, versus listed peers above 2x), with an established footprint of over 3.7 million HPs and ~910k HCs. The company has selectively acquired smaller ISPs to enter new cities, capturing synergies with the mobile expansion, while recent transactions in the sector have provided clearer valuation references for ISPs. Against this backdrop, we also see optionality in Unifique becoming a potential target by an incumbent player in a broader consolidation scenario.

Mobile and convergence as the next leg of growth. Unifique entered the mobile segment in 2024, leveraging its own 5G rights in Santa Catarina and Rio Grande do Sul, and has since added spectrum in Paraná, São Paulo and northern states, considerably broadening its addressable market, although also adding a relevant layer of execution risk to the thesis. Convergent bundles already account for 85% of the mobile base in 2Q26, contributing to historically low churn levels and to cost dilution over time. We forecast mobile reaching ~22% of total revenue by 2030 (from ~5% currently), driving marginal growth in the coming years, while the shareholder remuneration announced for 2026-28 adds a favorable carry to the thesis.

What concerns us

A relevant investment cycle adds execution risk. The 5G obligations in Santa Catarina and Rio Grande do Sul run through 2029, and the expansion into Paraná, São Paulo and the northern states takes the company into regions where it is still building presence. In the short term, the mobile ramp-up carries lower margins and weighs on consolidated profitability, partially offsetting the operating leverage gains from scale and efficiency initiatives.

A more competitive broadband environment adds uncertainty to the organic growth prospects. The ISP environment remains highly competitive across regions, and recent acquisitions bring integration efforts and incremental G&A until synergies materialize. As the company expands into new markets, sustaining the service quality that anchors its differentiated positioning becomes increasingly important for the thesis. In addition, as signaled by the company in its 2Q26 conference call, competition in broadband has recently intensified, increasing the uncertainty around the organic pace of net additions and price pass-through going forward.

What we do not know

  • The potential of the recently acquired markets is still difficult to estimate. The spectrum acquired in Paraná, São Paulo and the northern states considerably broadens the addressable market, but the commercial ramp-up in these regions is at an early stage, and the share Unifique can capture outside its core market is still uncertain.
  • The pace of the consolidation agenda is difficult to anticipate. M&A is central to the story, both as consolidator and as a potential target, but the timing, size and valuation of future transactions are beyond the company's full control, and larger moves could change the current capital structure.
  • The long-term margin profile depends on the maturation of the mobile operation. Mobile still runs at lower profitability levels, and the pace at which convergence gains offset this pressure will shape the company's structural margin over time.
