What the market data shows

Anatel's monthly data suggests that the competitive environment in postpaid has become more contested since late 2025. Using the regulator's public access and portability databases (link), we tracked the three major operators through June 2026, and a consistent pattern emerges across the main metrics we track: i) postpaid (ex-M2M and dongles) net additions diverged from 4Q25, with Claro accelerating and TIM slowing; ii) prepaid bases are shrinking at very different speeds, with TIM losing lines fastest while Vivo has recently turned positive; iii) number portability shifted in the same period, in the same direction; and iv) the share shifts have been more pronounced in the states where TIM is strongest. The period also coincides with NuCel's plan revamp and distribution expansion in October, a development we cover in detail in our accompanying update (link); here, our focus is on what the market data itself reveals.

Taken together, the numbers support the reading that postpaid competition has intensified, with asymmetric effects across the three operators. Vivo has held volumes and share broadly stable through the more competitive period; Claro has been the clear share winner since late 2025; and TIM has been the most affected across postpaid, prepaid and portability, with the pressure more visible in its strongest regions. In our view, this is the most objective evidence available that the competitive environment has become more challenging, and it sets the road map for the sections below: we look at postpaid and prepaid separately, then at portability as a cross-check, and finally at where, geographically, the shift is taking place.

Postpaid (ex-M2M and dongles) — diverging paths

Throughout this report, all postpaid figures refer to postpaid ex-M2M and dongles.

Postpaid momentum has diverged materially across the three operators since late 2025. Through most of 2025, each operator's monthly net additions ran at a reasonably stable pace; over the final months of the year and into 2026, the paths separated, with Claro stepping up and TIM softening. Claro's monthly net additions (postpaid ex-M2M and dongles) averaged ~245k in 1H25, moved to 426k in October 2025 and have sustained a materially higher level since then (~353k on average from October through June). TIM moved in the opposite direction, from an average of ~119k per month in 1H25 to ~71k in 1H26, with January (+32k) and June (+41k) the softest readings of the period. Vivo, in turn, remained broadly stable, adding between ~234k and ~369k per month throughout the period.

The cumulative effect has been a gradual redistribution of postpaid share toward Claro. Monthly differences of this magnitude compound over time, and twelve months of them are now visible in the share data. TIM's postpaid (ex-M2M and dongles) share declined 0.8pp (from 21.3% to 20.5%), with base growth decelerating gradually since November 2025, from +6.4% to +4.9% YoY. Claro gained 0.5pp (to 34.2%), with growth accelerating from +8.0% to +10.3% YoY. Vivo ceded 0.6pp (to 42.4%) while keeping growth stable at ~7% YoY. As a result, the growth gap between Claro and TIM widened from 1.8pp to 5.4pp over the period.

Postpaid, however, is only the destination of the migration flow. To understand how it is being fed, the prepaid segment deserves its own look.

Prepaid — the top of the funnel

In prepaid, the competitive question is not who is growing, but who is losing customers more slowly. The segment has been shrinking structurally for years, as customers migrate to control plans, and the three incumbents shed roughly 5.6mn prepaid lines combined over the last twelve months. What matters for the competitive reading is how that decline is distributed: TIM lost ~2.5mn lines (with December alone at -588k), Vivo lost ~2.1mn and Claro ~1.1mn. In share terms, TIM ceded 1.0pp of the prepaid market over twelve months (to 30.2%), while Claro gained 0.7pp (to 33.3%) and Vivo was broadly flat (34.2%).

The most recent readings suggest Vivo has found traction at the entry level, while TIM's trends have yet to stabilize. Vivo posted positive prepaid net additions in each of the last three months (+132k, +128k and +55k) and was the only operator in positive territory in June, suggesting its credit-card entry plans are capturing customers at the top of the funnel; Claro's trajectory also improved substantially (twelve-month losses narrowed from a peak of -3.6mn in October to -1.1mn, with positive readings between February and May); and TIM has yet to show a similar improvement, with negative net additions in five of the six months of 2026. For TIM, the most prepaid-oriented of the three, the combination is more challenging: a smaller prepaid pool, and more alternatives along the migration path, reduce the number of candidates to migrate into control, the segment where its net additions have softened.

Postpaid and prepaid measure the stock of customers each operator holds. Portability adds a flow reading: it captures the customers who actively choose to switch operators, keeping their number.

Portability — a cross-check on switching behavior

Brazilian mobile customers are switching operators in growing numbers. Portability captures the customers who actively choose to change providers while keeping their number; one caveat is that the figures combine prepaid and postpaid lines, so they do not isolate the postpaid discussion above, but they offer the most direct measure of competitive intensity, and one that does not depend on methodological interpretation. On that measure, the market has clearly become more active: 5.85mn lines were ported over the last twelve months, up 15% from 5.08mn in the prior twelve-month period, with May 2026 the highest month of the period analyzed (530k).

Claro has been the main recipient of that switching activity, with the shift emerging in 4Q25. Claro's monthly net balance ran at ~50k through September 2025, doubled to ~104k in October and has remained elevated since then. TIM, a net donor throughout the series, saw its monthly balance widen from roughly -95k/-115k in 1H25 to -140k/-151k in recent months, driven mainly by higher outflows: lines ported out rose from ~175k per month in 1H25 to as much as 231k in May 2026, while lines ported in remained broadly stable at ~65k-85k. Vivo oscillated around neutral and has recovered to a positive balance in recent months, helped by stronger gross inflows (168k in May). On a cumulative twelve-month basis, Claro gained ~985k lines via portability, Vivo ~+84k, and TIM's balance stood at ~-1.56mn, widening gradually over the period.

The bilateral flows suggest the pressure on TIM is broad-based rather than tied to a single competitor. Breaking TIM's twelve-month balance down by counterparty, the balance against Claro widened from -557k (twelve months to Jun/25) to -832k (twelve months to Jun/26), a 49% acceleration, with the monthly gap stepping up after October; the balance against Vivo moved from -421k to -497k; and TIM also shows negative balances against Brisanet (-69k), Unifique (-51k) and the remaining operators (-115k). The acceleration was not limited to TIM's flows: Vivo's twelve-month balance against Claro also widened (from -96k to -248k), and all three national operators show growing deficits against Brisanet. We would not overstate the read for Vivo, which has continued to hold share overall (including in São Paulo, its largest market, where it gained share over the period); the point is rather that Claro's portability gains are drawn from across the market, not from a single donor. In our view, the breadth of these flows is consistent with the reading from the stock data and with a more competitive environment overall.

These flows also have a clear geography, which is where we turn next.

The regional picture — pressure more visible in TIM's strongest markets

The competitive pressure has been most visible in the states where TIM holds its highest share. Aggregate national figures can hide where the battle is actually being fought, which is why the state-level cut is informative. Comparing postpaid share by state between June 2025 and June 2026, TIM ceded share in 17 of the 27 states and in the ten largest markets, and the declines are more pronounced where TIM's share is highest, with Paraná (47.6% share, -2.58pp), Santa Catarina (37.5%, -3.14pp) and Ceará (22.9%, -2.50pp) showing the largest variations. Claro, in turn, gained share in nine of the ten largest markets over the same period, while Vivo's variations were smaller and mixed.

Regional players are already moving the needle in specific markets, and Ceará is the clearest example. It is the only market among the ten largest in which all three major operators ceded share simultaneously, and the three combined hold ~81% of the state's postpaid base, against 93% to 99% in the other nine largest markets, which points to share gains by regional players. That is consistent with Brisanet's strength in the Northeast, and it leads directly into the next layer of the competitive discussion.

New entrants — additional pressure over the coming years

Beyond the three national operators, a second layer of competition is building. Brisanet and Unifique, regional ISPs that acquired 5G spectrum rights in the 2021 auction, are converting their fiber footprints into mobile businesses: their postpaid bases grew ~90% and ~101% YoY, respectively, and together they already account for ~1.2pp of postpaid share, with trailing 12-month net portability gains of ~164k and ~110k lines. The absolute numbers remain small, but the direction matters. As these operators expand 5G deployment and mobile coverage over their regional footprints (Brisanet in the Northeast, Unifique in the South), they should add a further layer of pressure at the local level over the coming years, in regions that overlap with areas where TIM holds elevated share.

The fringe is scaling on more than one front. In the MVNO space, the structural signals are clear even if aggregate line counts deserve care (licensee profiles vary widely, and several are IoT and M2M connectivity providers rather than consumer operators): the number of active licensees rose from 88 to 151 in three years, and the aggregate base quadrupled over the same period, from ~1.6mn lines in Jun/23 to ~6.3mn in Jun/26 (+49% in the last year alone). Notably, part of that infrastructure is provided by the incumbents themselves: the base hosted on Claro's network rose from ~354k to ~1.39mn lines in twelve months, driven by NuCel, which surpassed one million lines roughly a year and a half after launch and which we discuss in our accompanying update. In fixed broadband, the dashboard shows the same phenomenon from a different angle: Starlink's base accelerated from ~12k monthly additions in 1H25 to an average of ~44k per month since October 2025, reaching ~836k accesses (~1.5% of the national base). Adding the pieces together, smaller operators, virtual operators and satellite entrants already account for a growing slice of every access segment, and all of them are expanding at rates far above the market.

Looking ahead, two things will tell us whether the pressure is peaking or still building. The first is front-book pricing in the control segment, where our channel checks suggest the initiative rests with the postpaid market leaders. The second is the most recent data itself: in June 2026, Claro's postpaid net additions (+266k) and portability balance (+57k) moderated back toward pre-October levels, while Vivo's portability improved for a fourth consecutive month. One month does not make a trend, but it is the first sign of possible normalization in the series, and we will be watching the next readings closely.
