IT Services — a recovery that keeps slipping: downgrading Globant, staying with CI&T

Prices as of [XX-Jun-2026]; figures in USD unless noted. Coverage: Globant (GLOB, Market Perform, YE26 TP US$ [XX]) and CI&T (CINT, Outperform, YE26 TP US$ 7.00). Draft for internal review.

We are downgrading Globant to Market Perform and reiterating our Outperform rating on CI&T. We began 2026 expecting a gradual demand recovery for IT services, with the sector well positioned to capture the derivative of AI implementation inside enterprises. Six months in, that recovery has not materialized: companies have postponed investment decisions amid macro uncertainty, visibility on AI returns remains low, and the latest round of global peer results — Accenture above all — suggests the recovery may be slower than investors were beginning to expect. This backdrop reduces the odds of upside to Globant's already cautious FY26 guidance, at a point when the stock's main driver — growth — is absent; we are moving to the sidelines. CI&T sits on the other side of the same industry: six consecutive quarters of double-digit FXN organic growth and a guidance raise in May keep it as our preferred pick in the segment.

The sector: the recovery keeps slipping

2026 is not playing out as we expected. Our constructive view for the year rested on a simple sequence: enterprises would move AI from pilots to production, and the engineering work required — integration, data, legacy modernization — would land in the backlog of IT services providers. The first half broke that sequence. Adoption is happening, but it is converting into services revenue more slowly than we anticipated, and the industry ended the semester with decelerating bookings, guidance cuts and restructuring programs rather than the long-awaited inflection. In our view, two factors explain the delay.

First, macro: companies are postponing investment decisions. Amid tariffs, higher rates and the geopolitical conflict — a headwind we monitor without overstating — enterprises have deferred precisely the type of large, discretionary technology program that feeds services backlog. The US data illustrates the point: roughly two-thirds of US CFOs said economic uncertainty would lead them to delay or cut capex and hiring, with corporate IT among the most-cited budget lines for cuts (Deloitte CFO Signals, 1Q26). The pattern repeats in this quarter's transcripts across providers: longer decision-making cycles, stricter procurement, and approved projects ramping more slowly.

Second, lack of visibility on returns. The technology is still evolving, and new launches take time to prosper — both of our covered companies have rolled out their own AI-native delivery platforms (detailed in the company sections below), but embedding them in client workflows and proving ROI takes quarters, not weeks; proofs of concept must become production workloads before they move any provider's P&L. Most enterprises cannot yet demonstrate that payback: only 15% of AI decision-makers report an EBITDA lift from AI, and fewer than a third can tie AI's value to P&L changes (Forrester). While the proof doesn't come, budgets wait — Forrester expects large organizations to defer roughly a quarter of planned AI spending into 2027. Beneath the delay sits the more structural fear: AI is deflationary for effort-based delivery. Productivity gains flow quickly into rate cards and discounts, while the offsetting shifts in monetization — outcome- and consumption-based pricing — advance at procurement speed, not at technology speed. Gartner already frames the theme as revenue cannibalization of labor-intensive, high-margin services; IDC projects that 30% of contractual engagements will be outcome-based by 2030. The direction of travel is clear; the transition income statement is not.

The spending mix confirms the diagnosis. Worldwide IT spending should grow 13.5% in 2026, to US$ 6.31 trillion — but the money is flowing around services, not into them. IT services should grow 9.0%, the slowest segment except communication services, while data-center systems grow 55.8% and software 15.1% (Gartner, Apr-26). It is a multi-speed market: hyperscaler infrastructure and AI-centric software capture the AI budget today, and the services layer only monetizes later in the cycle, once implementation scales.

Peer results: the sector's thermometer still lacks visibility

Fragmented sector, but a readable sentiment. IT services is a fragmented industry, and client books are heterogeneous — exposure varies by geography, vertical, contract type and the split between discretionary projects and managed services, which is why results rarely move in lockstep. Even so, each earnings season works as a thermometer: read together, the prints reveal a sentiment that no single company can. This season, the reading was unambiguous — and more cautious into 2027. The same themes repeat across transcripts regardless of business model: decelerating bookings and book-to-bill at or below 1x, deals deferred rather than cancelled, discretionary work cut first, guidance reset lower, restructuring programs to defend margins, and buybacks doing the work that growth used to do. We run through the most relevant prints below.

Accenture was the cold shower. We see Accenture as the most relevant thermometer for global IT services demand, with ~4% market share — which is why its fiscal 3Q26 print moved the entire sector. Revenue of US$ 18.7 billion (+6% USD / +3% cc) came in slightly above the midpoint of guidance but below consensus; the real damage was in bookings, at US$ 19.3 billion, down 2% YoY and 13% QoQ — a sharp deceleration from +6% growth in the prior quarter, with consulting growing just 1% cc. More importantly, the company lowered its FY26 revenue growth guidance to 3–4% cc from 3–5%, attributing the revision to four factors: the Middle East conflict (~US$ 100 million of Q3 revenue lost, plus ~US$ 400 million of sales impact in the region), longer decision-making cycles in EMEA, two large managed-services deals deferred to FY27, and the US federal headwind. The stock fell 18%, its worst single-day drop on record; GLOB fell 6% in sympathy and Indian IT names slumped up to 7%. Our take: given that Accenture is perceived as the key proxy for IT services demand, its guidance reset suggests the sector's recovery may be slower than investors were beginning to expect — a negative read-through for the space in general, and for the debate around upside risk to Globant's guidance in particular. Notably, management flagged that AI infrastructure spending has not yet translated into services demand, though it should eventually drive incremental consumption as clients grapple with token costs and ROI realization — the same sequencing that anchors our sector view.

Endava shows what happens to discretionary digital work in this tape. Among listed peers, Endava is the closest business-model comparison to the LatAm digital-engineering names — mid-sized, project-based, discretionary — and its fiscal 3Q26 was the most dramatic print of the season: revenue fell 8.4% YoY (−6.4% cc), the adjusted PBT margin collapsed to 1.8% from 12.6%, the company recognized a £364.6 million goodwill impairment, and FY26 guidance was reset to a 5.0–6.0% cc decline. Management described the period as one of the most challenging in years, with clients scrutinizing technology spending more carefully than at any point since the macro slowdown began. The message for the space is uncomfortable: when budgets tighten, the project-based digital book — the same profile of work that drove the sector's 2020–22 boom — is the first line cut.

DXC confirms that the pressure extends into FY27. The legacy-heavy end of the industry is contracting outright: fiscal 4Q organic revenue fell 6.6% YoY, bookings dropped 13.5%, and the full-year book-to-bill of 0.98x left the company guiding FY27 revenue to US$ 12.11–12.35 billion — another annual decline. The stock fell 21.5% on the release. DXC's problems are partly self-inflicted, but a book-to-bill below 1x at a US$ 12+ billion revenue base tells us the replacement demand for large managed-services contracts is simply not showing up — consistent with the deferrals Accenture reported.

Wipro put an explicit contraction on the table for FY27. The company guided its 1Q FY27 to −2.0% to 0% cc — an outright sequential decline to open the fiscal year — with 4Q bookings down 13.9% YoY and large-deal TCV down 18.5%. Management attributed the caution to macro uncertainty, client indecision and slower deal cycles, with financial services clients particularly conservative outside India. The Rs 15,000 crore buyback announced alongside works as a valuation floor, not as a growth answer — a capital-allocation playbook we increasingly see across the sector, Globant included.

Cognizant is restructuring its way through the transition. The 1Q26 numbers beat estimates, but the more telling disclosure was Project Leap: US$ 230–320 million in charges, mostly severance, targeting US$ 200–300 million in annual savings. In our reading, this is the industry acknowledging that AI-enabled delivery requires structurally fewer people — margins are being defended by cost reduction, not supported by growth. Despite the operational beat, the stock trades near 52-week lows, down 33% YTD: the market is not paying for cost programs in a growth-starved sector.

Infosys anchored expectations for a weak 2027. The quarter itself beat (revenue of US$ 5.04 billion vs. US$ 4.98 billion expected), but FY27 guidance of +1.5% to +3.5% cc came in below the ~2–5% the market anticipated, and large-deal TCV decelerated to US$ 3.2 billion from US$ 4.8 billion in the prior quarter. Management pointed to slower decision-making and clients reassessing tech budgets in light of AI productivity opportunities — deflation showing up as a demand argument. For us, this was the clearest early signal that the recovery debate had shifted from "2H26" to "some point in 2027."

The common thread: this is an industry-wide condition, not an idiosyncratic one. Across six of the most relevant global prints of the season, the pattern repeats — bookings decelerating, guidance cut or reset lower, restructuring programs multiplying, and buybacks doing the work that growth used to do. This matters for how we read Globant: the deterioration is a sector condition, which supports our view that patience will be required across the space. But it also raises the bar for any individual name to outgrow it — which makes CI&T's numbers, below, all the more notable.

Our view: a fragmented market that still rewards differentiation — but a longer wait. IT services remains a fragmented market, with performance varying by client base, vertical exposure and geography, and we continue to believe that providers with genuine know-how, proprietary delivery platforms and the ability to optimize token economics are well positioned for the implementation cycle, which is still very early stage. Lower unit costs should ultimately expand the number of viable AI use cases rather than shrink the workload. But we recognize the recovery is taking longer than we expected, and we prefer to anchor 2027 on the cautious base peers have already set — Infosys at +1.5–3.5% cc, Wipro opening the year in contraction, DXC declining — rather than underwrite a reacceleration we cannot yet see in bookings. In the meantime, the sector's de-rating has been indiscriminate, while operational performance has not: the momentum decoupling between CI&T and Globant is the widest in our coverage history, and it drives the rating actions below.

Globant: downgrading to Market Perform

The downgrade comes down to growth. Globant compounded revenue at 37% in 2022 and 18% in 2023, slowed to +1.6% in 2025, and guides FY26 to +0.3% to +2.2% (midpoint of US$ 2,485 million, +1% YoY). 1Q26 net revenue of US$ 607.1 million fell 0.7% YoY — the second consecutive quarterly decline — even coming in 1% above our estimate and above the high end of the company's guidance. Our model sits at the bottom of the range, at US$ 2,484 million (+1.2% YoY) for 2026 and +3.5% for 2027. Growth is the main driver investors like in this case; without it, a re-rating is hard to justify, however cheap the stock looks.

The 2Q26 guidance was the trigger we were waiting for — and it came flat. Since our March update, we had flagged the 2Q26 guidance as the main variable to reassess the thesis, as it should indicate a modest sequential rebound in growth. The company guided 2Q26 revenue to US$ 610–616 million — flat YoY at the midpoint. Meanwhile, the quality of growth narrowed: the top-50 accounts grew 5.2% YoY and the top 2-to-5 clients 8.2%, lifting top-10 concentration to 30.5% of revenues (from 29.1%), while the number of accounts above US$ 1 million fell to 333 from 341 — the core is holding, but the base is shrinking. Regionally, Europe led (+8% YoY) and LatAm rose 4%, while North America (54% of revenues) declined 4% and New Markets fell 7%; among verticals, Healthcare stood out (+17%) while Professional Services dropped 16% and Financial Services fell 6%. With the book still weighted toward effort-priced work (time-and-materials at ~64% of revenues, down from 68% a year ago), and Accenture signaling softer discretionary demand ahead, we see deceleration risks into 2H26 — a half the guidance assumes will be helped by more billable days and the ramp-up of recently onboarded clients.

Globant's exposure adds risk to the demand recovery. The Middle East accounts for ~4% of revenues and had been one of the few growth engines — the same disruption that cost Accenture ~US$ 100 million in revenue plus ~US$ 400 million in regional sales last quarter. Travel- and hospitality-related accounts add ~13% of revenues, a vertical sensitive to the same headwinds. Management noted it has not yet observed demand deceleration linked to geopolitical tensions in New Markets, but the region's revenue already fell 7% YoY in 1Q26 and, in our view, these exposures reduce the odds of upside to an already cautious guidance.

Globant is trying to ramp AI Pods, but the model is still very early stage. The 1Q26 disclosure showed sequential traction: ARR reached US$ 32.8 million as of March (from a US$ 20.6 million exit rate at YE25), the pipeline expanded to US$ 352 million (from US$ 283 million in 4Q25), and the model has been incorporated into 40% of the top-20 revenue-generating accounts (up from 30%), with gross margins running materially above the company's consolidated ~37%. The company targets an ARR exit rate of US$ 60–100 million for 2026. That said, the rollout still faces procurement and client-education bottlenecks, and against ~US$ 2.5 billion in revenue, AI Pods represent ~1.3% of the total — the hybrid people-plus-agents model is correctly aimed, but cannot yet offset the pressure on the traditional book.

The Anthropic alliance strengthens the narrative, not yet the numbers. On June 30, Globant announced a multi-year alliance with Anthropic, introducing Claude-powered AI Pods and joining the Claude Partner Network as one of the first Preferred Services Partners — the largest LatAm-born services company to do so. The agreement gives all 28,500 employees access to Claude, includes Forward Deploy Engineer certification programs (with a practice targeting 5,000 certified architects), and focuses precisely on the verticals where Globant is most exposed: media & entertainment, gaming, airlines and hospitality. We read the deal as a positive signal for the AI Pods pipeline and for Globant's positioning as an AI-native provider — but, like the AI Pods themselves, it is optionality on 2027+, not support for 2026 numbers.

Profitability and headcount tell the story of the transition. The 1Q26 non-GAAP EBIT margin of 14.1% (−70 bps YoY, within the 14–15% guidance) reflects FX headwinds and wage pressure in delivery hubs such as Colombia and India — which led us to cut our 2026 margin forecast by 110 bps to 14.4% in the March update. Headcount is now part of the equation: total professionals fell 8.3% YoY to 28,510, and we model a modest decline in 2026 followed by broadly stable levels in 2027, with revenue per IT professional rising ~6% as AI-enabled delivery scales. In other words, the P&L is being managed for productivity, not expansion — appropriate for the environment, but not the profile of a re-rating stock.

The cash position stands out, but does not restore growth. 1Q26 FCF reached US$ 36 million (vs. −US$ 6 million a year ago) on capex down to ~3% of revenues, and the company announced a new US$ 125 million buyback (~8.5% yield) to be executed over six quarters, after completing the previous program. We project FCF approaching US$ 254 million by YE26 — a ~13% FCF yield — helped by lower capex intensity and better working capital. At ~5.5x non-GAAP P/E 2026 (an ~84% discount to its 10-year average and ~41% below peers), valuation is clearly depressed; but cheapness is not a catalyst, and in this tape the balance sheet defends the floor rather than re-rating the stock.

This is a move to neutral, not a bear call. Globant retains a strong client base, real delivery capability and a management team that moved early on AI. What would bring us back: a guidance path marking a genuine growth inflection, AI Pods tracking to the upper half of the US$ 60–100 million ARR target, and stabilization in North America and Financial Services. Until growth is visible, the risk/reward no longer supports a positive rating.

CI&T: reiterating Outperform

The operational momentum is different. CI&T posted its sixth consecutive quarter of double-digit FXN organic growth in 1Q26: net revenue of US$ 136.6 million rose 23.2% YoY in USD (+15.5% FXN), 1% above our estimate and the company's own guidance. More importantly, the company raised FY26 guidance to 13.5–17.5% organic growth (US$ 555.8–575.3 million, from 12–16%), attributing 100 bps of the 150-bps midpoint revision to higher demand — not FX — and set a 2Q26 floor of "at least US$ 140 million" (+19.5% YoY), 5% above our forecast. Same macro, same AI wave — a materially better outcome.

Execution has been consistent, not lucky. The growth trajectory has improved sequentially across regions: North America accelerated from +6% YoY in 3Q25 to +12% in 4Q25 and +16% in 1Q26 despite the challenging demand backdrop, LatAm grew 33%, and New Markets inflected to +11% after declining 8.5% in 4Q25. Verticals are equally broad-based — Financial Services +37% and TMT +41% in 1Q26, after Financial Services led 4Q25 at +43%. Concentration risk is falling as growth broadens: the top client still grew 25% YoY (11% of revenues), but its contribution to marginal growth declined to ~11%, from 41% in 3Q25 and 72% in 2Q25 — a healthier growth mix, in our view. The pipeline is up ~30% YoY, driven by AI demand and platform differentiation.

CI&T monetizes the productivity gain rather than being cannibalized by it. Flow — with a library of ~2,000 agents — makes AI adoption practical, measurable and scalable for clients, and gives CI&T a clear way to demonstrate the value it creates (output per unit of effort), strengthening its position in client conversations. It works on two fronts: a delivery accelerator that wins AI-adoption work and gains share within existing clients, and a pricing lever that captures part of the efficiency it creates — 20% of 1Q26 new sales were already signed under outcome-based models, with the share expected to scale over the next 18 months as client procurement frameworks gradually adapt. The talent equation supports the model: attrition of ~10% is the lowest in the industry, and the company keeps hiring AI-native junior developers, which also helps offset FX pressure on gross margin.

The demand mix points to a longer runway. Legacy modernization and data initiatives represent the bulk of current activity — exactly the work that must precede any enterprise AI deployment — while management sees business-model reinvention, boosted by AI, as the next major growth wave. In a fragmented industry where horizontal solutions are more exposed to disruption, CI&T's positioning in high-value, custom transformations (AI agents, legacy modernization, workflow redesign) insulates it from commoditization. We also see optionality in selective M&A to accelerate its US and Mexico presence, given the discounted valuations across the industry — though management is appropriately disciplined on AI-related targets.

Margins are the swing factor, not the thesis. The 1Q26 adjusted EBITDA margin of 15.2% (−240 bps YoY, in line with our estimate) reflected typical 1Q seasonality, FX and a deliberate 26% increase in SG&A to sustain growth; the 17–19% FY26 guidance is unchanged. If outcome-based pricing scales as planned, better monetization of IP and token-related costs becomes an upside risk to margins — the mirror image of the deflation risk pressuring effort-priced peers.

Risks are the usual ones; valuation remains supportive. Top-client concentration (11% of revenues), FX, execution on the pricing transition and beta to further sector de-rating. None offsets the core point: trading at 10.3x/9.3x non-GAAP P/E for 2026/27 (~45% discount to its own history) while raising guidance as peers cut, CI&T strengthens its track record with each print and is well positioned to keep gaining relevance in the global IT services landscape. We reiterate our Outperform rating and YE26 target price of US$ 7.00/share; CINT remains our preferred pick in IT services.
