Brisanet — Restating coverage with Market Perform: balanced setup, cautious near-term view

Brisanet (BRST3) — Market Perform, TP BRL [XX] (YE27). Prices/estimates as of [XX-Aug-2026]; BRL unless noted. Draft for internal review.

Few stories in Brazilian telecom match Brisanet's: a company born in the interior of the Northeast that built, entirely organically, the region's largest fiber operation, and is now scaling a mobile business on top of it. In this context, we see a more balanced risk-reward at current levels, reflecting: i) exposure to a region with structurally challenging economics, such as relatively lower ARPU, higher churn levels, and a competitive broadband market with a high number of aggressive smaller broadband competitors; ii) a higher leverage position (~2.2x ND/EBITDA) that limits inorganic consolidation optionality and keeps interest expenses elevated amid the ongoing 5G rollout in the coming years; and iii) fewer near-term catalysts for a re-rating, considering that the Midwest expansion adds execution risk to the thesis, while the modest dividend yield (~[X]%) offers limited downside protection in a challenging macro scenario, with elevated interest rates that end up weighing on names with lower liquidity. Against this backdrop, we are restating our coverage of Brisanet with a Market Perform rating and a target price of BRL [XX]/share for 2027 (upside of [XX]%), based on a multiple of 3.5x EV/EBITDA for 2026E and [X.X]x for 2027.

A market leader built over more than two decades of regional execution. Founded more than 30 years ago to serve the interior of the Northeast, Brisanet scaled organically to become the largest ISP in the region, with over 7.2 million homes passed, ~1.6 million homes connected, and presence across every state in the region. Since then, the 2021 IPO funded both the expansion, currently with an 18.8% FTTH market share in the Northeast, and the entry into the mobile segment, following the win of 5G spectrum for the Northeast/Midwest that same year. The company has been diversifying its portfolio across broadband, mobile, TV and B2B corporate services (via the Agility franchise arm), focusing on increasing its take-up rate and bundled offerings in the coming years.

In broadband, leadership is consolidated, and the focus is shifting from footprint expansion to monetizing the existing infrastructure. Brisanet has established itself as the FTTH leader in seven of the nine Northeast states, ending 2Q26 with ~1.54 million FTTH HCs and ~7.2 million HPs, with a take-up rate of ~22% that the company now prioritizes expanding over new network build-out. Naturally, broadband is already a mature business (with higher margins than the consolidated level) with more selective marginal growth, and Brisanet has been favoring healthier ARPU levels (B2C ARPU +6.6% YoY) over faster subscriber growth, translating into softer net additions in recent months (~26k in 1H26, compared with ~67k in 1H25). Broadband churn has held broadly stable at ~2.1%, reflecting the price-sensitive consumer base and competitive dynamics of the region, while broadband revenue continues to grow at double digits despite the softer volumes, supported by the ARPU strategy and complemented by the ramp-up of the B2B corporate segment.

Mobile ramp-up is the main growth avenue, with 5G rollout obligations extending the investment cycle. Since starting mobile operations in late 2023, Brisanet has been leveraging its own 5G spectrum for the Northeast to build a convergence proposition anchored on mobile plus fiber bundles. The mobile base surpassed one million accesses by mid-2026 (1.07 million in June), and mobile gross revenue is approaching ~11% of consolidated gross revenue. However, the 5G spectrum obligations (running through 2029) require substantial capex deployment in the coming years, a trade-off that pressures free cash flow in the short term but should support operational leverage in the mid to long term.

Risks to monitor. In our view, four risks deserve closer attention: i) a competitive Northeast broadband market, still marked by relevant informal and smaller competitors, which limits pricing power; ii) the ~2.2x ND/EBITDA leverage position, which limits inorganic consolidation and keeps interest expenses elevated under a high-interest-rate macroeconomic environment; iii) capex intensity remaining high to meet the 5G rollout obligations through 2029, keeping FCF under pressure; and iv) execution risk in the mobile ramp-up outside the core Northeast footprint, particularly in the Midwest, and considering recent signs of a more promotional competitive environment in postpaid.

Brisanet offers a balanced setup, with limited near-term catalysts for a re-rating; Unifique remains as our preferred choice among ISPs. We recognize Brisanet's successful track record in the Northeast and the value of the asset built over the years. However, taking into account the execution risks associated with the Midwest expansion, a challenging macro environment, the uncertainties of the region and the company's leveraged balance sheet, we prefer to wait for a moment of greater visibility, supporting our Market Perform stance on BRST. Against this backdrop, Unifique remains our preferred choice among ISPs, considering its healthy operating metrics (churn, ARPU) in a premium region, its positioning as a potential consolidator in the South of the country, a favorable carry ([XX]% dividend yield for 2026), low leverage ([X.X]x ND/EBITDA) and the optionality of being a potential target in a scenario of consolidation in the Brazilian broadband sector.

Section 01 — Brisanet: restating coverage with Market Perform

In this section, we detail the building blocks behind our balanced stance. On the positive side, we highlight Brisanet's organic growth track record, a committed controlling family with a long-term horizon, and a mobile ramp-up that is gaining traction. On the cautious side, the structurally tougher economics of the Northeast and a maturing broadband market. Finally, we discuss the open questions that should shape the equity story ahead, from recurring capex levels to the potential of convergence.

What we like on the thesis

A rare track record of purely organic execution. Brisanet has grown entirely organically to date, becoming the largest ISP in the Northeast and expanding from 173k subscribers in small and mid-sized cities in 2018 to ~1.6 million homes connected today, with a vertically integrated model (own network, own towers, ~117k km of fiber and ~300 data centers) that supports both service quality and cost discipline. In the areas where it operates, network quality is a genuine competitive advantage, and the large incumbents have concentrated their fiber investments in denser markets, leaving the interior comparatively less contested.

The mobile ramp-up is working, and convergence is starting to show. Mobile operations started in late 2023, already cover ~50% of the Northeast, including five state capitals, and the base surpassed one million accesses with net additions running at a normalized ~40k per month; management sees ~2 million accesses by 2028 as an achievable target, with a longer-term ambition of ~15% share of the covered population. Convergent offers anchor the strategy (entry combo of 500Mb plus mobile at BRL 89.90, with the discount allocated to the mobile line), roughly 21% of the mobile base already holds fiber, and the 700MHz spectrum acquisition should reduce the number of sites required going forward. Mobile gross revenue is approaching a ~BRL 300 million annual run-rate.

Profitability and balance sheet are moving in the right direction. Adjusted EBITDA margin has held at 42-43% through the expansion cycle (management targets at least 42.5% for 2026, with some expansion in 2027 on personnel and marketing dilution), operating cash flow has consistently run at or above 100% of EBITDA, and leverage has been easing (2.17x, against a 2.5x internal target and a 3.5x covenant), supported by increasingly cheap subsidized funding lines (BNDES and regional development banks, with BRL 700-800 million in subsidized tranches yet to be drawn). The Midwest expansion, deliberately kept minimal for now (~BRL 70-100 million of the 2026 capex, 115 localities via an initial footprint), represents optionality rather than a bet the company depends on.

What concerns us

The region's economics are structurally tougher. The Northeast combines a lower average ticket, a more challenging consumer cycle and monthly broadband churn of ~2.1%, which together limit pricing power: management itself acknowledges that price pass-through is difficult, and that churn is partly what enables price increases. The competitive landscape adds to the pressure, with a long tail of small ISPs practicing irrational prices, a relevant informal segment operating below the regulatory radar, and enforcement that remains weak in practice, particularly on pole rentals. Occasionally, a large player enters with below-market prices for limited periods, adding noise to the front book.

Broadband, the cash engine, is decelerating. Net additions have slowed to ~5k per month (management guides to 15-18k per quarter, prioritizing consolidated profitability over volume), and Brisanet's FTTH share in the Northeast has drifted down over the last two years while the market fragments further. All of the company's cash flow today comes from fiber, precisely the segment that is maturing, while the mobile build-out still consumes capital: capex of ~BRL 720 million in 2026 (60-65% mobile) and ~BRL 700 million in 2027 keeps free cash flow near zero this year, with the inflection expected only in 2027-28.

Execution and regional risks are real, and some are unusual. The 5G ramp faced handset-compatibility issues that Anatel was slow to address, and the company has dealt with cable theft and infrastructure vandalism linked to criminal factions in Ceará, which delayed the convergence push (the situation has improved recently). Mobile pricing is also constrained early on (no pass-through in the first year of a customer's contract), the Midwest expansion adds execution risk outside the core footprint, part of the financial operations carries unhedged FX exposure, and the stock's low liquidity works against the name in the short term.

What we do not know

The structural questions that will define the equity story remain open. First, the level of recurring capex once the expansion cycle ends: management argues that most of the Northeast infrastructure is already built, but the company has yet to operate in a steady state, and the true maintenance capex level is a structural unknown. Second, the long-term tax rate, given Sudene benefits in some states and management's ~15% guidance, which carries uncertainty over time. Third, how much of the covered population the mobile operation can actually convert (the ~15% share ambition), and at what pricing, considering the first-year pass-through restriction. Fourth, whether the current competitive irrationality in Northeast broadband is cyclical or a permanent feature of a fragmented, partially informal market. And fifth, the pace at which convergence can reduce churn and sustain ARPU growth without sacrificing volume, the balance the company is explicitly trying to manage this year.
